# Research anchor: One Mississippi off-site receiver transaction

Updated: 2026-11-10

## Why this matters

David Allen's October 27, 2023 Planning Commission letter states that Trestle had already created San Diego's first "offsite receiver," satisfied the affordable obligation of its market-rate partner, and built 16 additional low-income units with supportive services within walking distance of the sender site.

The transaction is identifiable as One Mississippi / Mississippi ECB from SDHC records.

## Confirmed receiver

- Receiver: One Mississippi (formerly Mississippi ECB Apartments)
- Public project address: 4234 Mississippi Street; SDHC project materials also use 2139/2141 El Cajon Boulevard for the development.
- Community: North Park / Greater North Park
- Developer: Trestle Development / Trestle Mississippi LP
- Total residential units: 61 including one manager unit
- Affordable units: 60

## Confirmed market-rate counterparties / sending developments

SDHC HCR20-050 identifies two market-rate developments whose Density Bonus obligations were to be satisfied at Mississippi ECB:

1. Floit Properties, Inc. — 4469-4571 Ohio Street, San Diego.
2. Lennar — 4355 Park Boulevard, San Diego.

Floit and Lennar provided a combined $9.6 million loan/off-site contribution through National Housing Corporation. In exchange, Trestle agreed to construct affordable units satisfying the City Density Bonus requirements for those two market-rate developments.

## Unit arithmetic requiring source confirmation

Allen later described 16 units as additional to the market-rate obligation. One Mississippi contains 60 affordable units. If both statements refer to this transaction, the two sender obligations total 44 units (60 - 16). This is a derived reconciliation, not yet a sourced allocation split.

Do not assign the 44 units between Ohio Street and 4355 Park Boulevard until an SDHC agreement, recorded addendum, density-bonus schedule, permit condition, or equivalent source provides the split.

## Research significance for The Refuge

This establishes an earlier Trestle transaction pattern:
market-rate developer contribution -> Trestle receiver project -> excess affordable units created through LIHTC/bond financing.

Search the Refuge counterparties using the same evidence vocabulary and entity patterns: developer/owner names, off-site contribution/loan, Density Bonus or CCHS obligation, SDHC agreement, recorded addendum/deed restriction, receiver project, and project address.
